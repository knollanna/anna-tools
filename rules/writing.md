# Writing

This applies to everything: site copy, commit messages, PR bodies, READMEs, docs, cover
letters, and replies in a chat session. It started as a voice pass on annaknoll.com in
August 2026 and held up well enough to promote.

American English throughout.

## The bar

**Prefer one checkable detail over one balanced sentence.**

"Coached them into people other teams request by name" earns its place. "Turns complexity
into clarity" does not. The first can be verified by someone who was there. The second is a
shape that sounds like insight and asserts nothing.

When you catch yourself reaching for a phrase because it sounds finished, replace it with
something that could be wrong.

## The tells to cut

These are LLM habits. They are the easiest thing in Anna's writing to undo by accident,
which is exactly why they are written down.

| Habit | Why it goes |
| --- | --- |
| **"Not X, but Y"** antithesis | The most common machine tell. Reads as profundity, carries one idea. Cut to Y. The site went 9 → 2. |
| **"Actually" / "genuinely" / "truly"** as intensifiers | Authenticity signals that signal the opposite. The site went 5 → 0. |
| **Abstraction-to-abstraction claims** | "Complexity into clarity", "insight into action", "vision to reality". Name the actual thing. |
| **Em dashes as default punctuation** | Fine occasionally, exhausting in bulk. The work page went 21 → 5. Use a period or a comma. |
| **Tricolon** ("faster, cleaner, and more maintainable") | Three items because three sounds complete, not because there are three. |
| **Hedge stacking** ("it's worth noting that it may be helpful to") | Say the thing or don't. |
| **Closing summaries that restate the opening** | If the reader needs it, the middle failed. |
| **"Great question", "Absolutely", "You're right to ask"** | Filler acknowledgement. Start with the answer. |

## Voice

- **First person, past tense for achievements.** "I built a team of ten technical architects"
  claims the achievement without claiming the current seat. That distinction is deliberate.
- **Sentence case for titles and headings.** Not Title Case, not ALL CAPS.
- **Plain-spoken register.** The site's own line for this is "In plain terms: I turn 'that
  sounds complicated' into 'let's do it.'" Match that.
- **The computational-linguistics plus enterprise-architecture combination is the standout.**
  It is unusual and it is hers. Give it room instead of flattening it into "technical
  background".
- **Don't name a narrow current role in a headline.** "Technical architects" was rejected on
  the site as too limiting. Let the eyebrow or the role line carry the title so the headline
  can carry the point of view.

## In conversation

Same rules. Concretely, in a session with Anna:

- Lead with the answer. Context after, if it's needed.
- Don't restate her question back to her.
- Don't pad with acknowledgements or close with a summary of what was just said.
- When something is uncertain, say which part and why. Don't hedge the whole statement.
- Don't apologize repeatedly or narrate a correction at length. Fix it and continue.

## What stays private

- **No job-search details in anything public.** This was a standing rule for the site and it
  still holds.
- No client names, no employer material. See the non-negotiables in `CLAUDE.md`.
- Affiliate links on annaknoll.com keep every tracking param verbatim — `partner_id`, `pid`,
  `uid`, `mcid`, `cmp`. Stripping one breaks the commission. Instagram `?igsh=` share params
