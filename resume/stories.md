# Anna Knoll — stories

STAR anecdotes. The things she would actually tell someone in an interview or use as the spine
of a cover letter.

**This is the highest-value file here and the one nobody writes.** A skills list produces
mush. A real story produces a real letter. It is worth an hour of her time and cannot be
generated for her.

> **Every story below is a prompt, not a story.** The handles and the "known" lines come from
> the resume or from Anna. The content is hers to fill. Do not invent, embellish, or
> reconstruct one. If a letter needs an anecdote and none is filled in, ask.

## Format

```
### <Short handle — how Anna refers to it>
**Use for:** <leadership | ambiguity | conflict | technical depth | failure | influence>
**Situation:** where, when, what was at stake
**Task:** what she specifically owned
**Action:** what she did, in order, including the call that could have gone wrong
**Result:** what changed, with a number if there is one
**What she'd say differently now:**
```

Two things make these work:

1. **One checkable detail beats one balanced sentence.** "Coached them into people other teams
   request by name" is the bar. See `rules/writing.md`.
2. **The story needs a moment where it could have gone the other way.** A story with no risk in
   it is a summary.

## Leadership and building teams

### The team of ten
**Use for:** leadership, scaling, hiring
**Known:** leads 10 Technical Architects in HLS at Salesforce; built it through strategic
hiring and onboarding.
**Needs:** what existed before it. The first hire and why. The hire that was wrong. What the
team can do at ten that it could not do at three.

### The specialist team from scratch
**Use for:** ambiguity, standing something up, coaching
**Known:** stood up and led a newly hired specialist team for Employee Experience products in
HLS (2018–2022), coaching multiple SEs with no prior Salesforce experience.
**Needs:** how she picked people with no platform background. The specific coaching
intervention. Who later requested them by name, for what, and how she found out. This is the
"coached into demand" line on the site — it needs its one engineer.

### The POC program
**Use for:** operational design, influence without authority, AI in practice
**Known:** launched and operationalized the team owning customer POC success for Agentforce
and Data 360. Designed the intake process, defined evaluation criteria, triaged requests to
resources.
**Needs:** what was going wrong before intake existed. The evaluation criterion that was
contentious. A POC she declined and what happened. **This is the most directly relevant story
to the forward-deployed move and to the SA→FDE handoff thesis.** Worth writing first.

### Founding the ERG
**Use for:** persistence, influence, values
**Known:** spent over a year advocating with Salesforce leadership to establish a Jewish ERG.
Launched early 2025. Now Global VP for ~1,500 members across US, EMEA, LATAM, Israel.
**Needs:** what the year of advocacy actually consisted of. The objection she had to answer
most often. The moment it turned.

## Technical depth

### Linguistics to engineering
**Use for:** the origin story, differentiation, why-you
**Known:** BA General Linguistics (Hebrew University, 2001), sociolinguistics research on
code-switching → computational linguistics coursework (SDSU, 2006) → NLP consulting at InQuira
and Oracle. Ontologies, taxonomies, enterprise search, search accuracy tuning.
**Needs:** one concrete hand-tuning problem she still remembers. Why this makes her good at
the current wave rather than a charming detail. **This is the bridge from her background to AI
work and the thing that differentiates her in a room full of pre-sales leaders.**

### The Oracle Knowledge upgrade
**Use for:** delivery under pressure, technical ownership
**Known:** led Infogain's first complete Oracle Knowledge upgrade while running concurrent
enterprise search engagements across multiple clients.
**Needs:** what made it the first. What broke. What she would have scoped differently.

### Teaching NLP in two languages

**Known:** developed training materials and taught in English and Russian at InQuira; authored
their first non-English software documentation.
**Needs:** why it fell to her. What was hard about it beyond translation — a terminology

### The transcript analysis tool

**Known:** built a tool using Claude that indexed hundreds of meeting transcripts by
participant and date, enabling natural-language querying across six months of conversations.
**Needs:** the problem that made it worth building. What it found that she would have missed.

### Shipping FareWatch
**Use for:** technical depth, building solo, product judgment

**Needs:** the hardest call. The bug that cost the most. Why she built it at all.

## Judgment and standards

### Holding a standard under pressure
**Use for:** quality, conviction, saying no
**Known:** set WCAG AAA (7:1) on her own site, above the usual 4.5:1 floor, and rejected a
whole design direction whose premise — "color carries hierarchy instead of size" — conflicted
with SC 1.4.1. Then had the contrast pairs asserted at build time so the standard could not
quietly slip.
**Needs:** the professional version, where holding the line had a cost and someone pushed back.

### The voice pass
**Use for:** judgment, editing, working with AI
**Known:** in August 2026 cut a family of machine-written habits from her own site — "not X,
but Y" from 9 instances to 2, "actually"/"genuinely" from 5 to 0, em dashes from 21 to 5 on one
page — then wrote down the rule so it would not creep back.
**Needs:** almost nothing. Nearly complete already, and an unusually good answer to "how do you
actually use AI?" because it is about knowing what to reject.

### Kivunim
**Use for:** values, long engagements, architecture for real constraints
**Known:** four years pro bono designing and implementing a Salesforce solution for a nonprofit
serving young adults with physical disabilities — intake, program matching, participation
monitoring, donation management.
**Needs:** the constraint that made it different from enterprise work. What she built that she
is proudest of. Who maintains it now.
